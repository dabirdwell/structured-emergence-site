There's a product specification sitting in our vault right now: 686 lines of design vision for a critical thinking trainer called Dojo. It maps logical fallacies to martial arts belt levels and structures five distinct training modes around argument quality rather than rhetorical persuasion.

The core insight, backed by research: argument mapping-based instruction produces critical thinking gains of 0.70 to 0.80 standard deviations. That's equivalent to moving someone from the 50th to the 79th percentile. Twice the gains of standard critical thinking courses.

<!--more-->

## The Belt System

The fallacy taxonomy progresses like martial arts:

**White Belt** introduces the intuitive fallacies: Ad Hominem, Straw Man, Appeal to Authority, Appeal to Emotion. You can spot these in everyday conversation without training.

**By Green Belt**, you're detecting what's *missing* from arguments: False Dilemma, Slippery Slope, Red Herring. The absence is harder to see than the presence.

**Brown Belt** requires formal logic and quantitative reasoning: base rate neglect, survivorship bias, Simpson's paradox. These are the fallacies that fool experts.

**Black Belt** means you can identify compound fallacies in real-world rhetoric and teach others. Mastery isn't just recognition. It's the ability to construct the strongest possible version of an argument you disagree with.

## Steel Man Over Straw Man

The second training mode is called Steel Man. You receive a position statement on a real issue. Your job isn't to demolish it. Your job is to construct the *strongest possible* argument *for* that position, even if you disagree.

The AI evaluates on three dimensions: charity (did you represent it as proponents would recognize?), evidence (did you find the strongest supporting reasoning?), and steelmanning (did you make it *stronger* than the original?).

This might be the single most important skill for democratic discourse. If you can't articulate why someone disagrees with you in terms they would accept, you don't understand the issue.

## Scoring Logic, Not Rhetoric

The signature mode (the reason someone tells their friends about the app) is a live debate against an AI opponent. But the scoring is inverted from what you'd expect. It doesn't score persuasion. It scores logic.

Did your conclusions follow from your premises? Did you cite verifiable facts? Did you commit any fallacies? Did you address the opponent's actual arguments? Did you acknowledge the strongest version of the opposing view?

The AI deliberately uses fallacies at lower belt levels so you learn to spot them mid-conversation. At higher levels, it argues with full logical rigor. It never uses personal attacks or emotional manipulation. It models respectful disagreement.

## Why This Matters Now

We live in a moment when the tools of persuasion are becoming extraordinarily powerful and the tools of reasoning are not keeping pace. Large language models can generate compelling arguments for any position in seconds. The ability to evaluate those arguments (to know when you're being manipulated, to distinguish evidence from assertion, to construct counter-arguments with logical rigor) is becoming as essential as reading.

Our reading trainer teaches you to *read*. Our critical thinking trainer teaches you to *think clearly*. The civic hub we're building teaches you to *participate*. The pipeline builds capacity layer by layer: comprehension, reasoning, civic engagement.

Dojo is the reasoning layer. And it's designed to be genuinely fun, not gamification as manipulation, but gamification as *informational feedback about genuine competence growth*. The difference matters.
