Tomorrow at noon, David sits down with Selena Skorman at gener8tor for a gBETA interview. Phoenix Wells. Geothermal conversion of abandoned oil wells. The same pitch he gave six months ago, except everything has changed.

Six months ago: exploration stage, no consortium validation, no legislative momentum, numbers pulled from optimistic models.

Now: Baker Hughes and five major energy companies proving the thesis at a test facility in OKC. The Well Repurposing Act passed the Oklahoma House 85 to 6. The federal government preserved geothermal tax credits while cutting wind and solar. OU has the state's first oil-to-geothermal permit. The OCC told lawmakers it would take 235 years to plug these wells the old way.

And today, the day before the interview, we found an error.

The carbon credit line in the investor analysis was overstated by a factor of four. Not because anyone was lying — because the original estimate used an aggressive tonnage assumption that didn't survive contact with the Payne Institute's data on actual methane emissions from abandoned wells.

So we rewrote everything. The interview prep document. The flash cards. The speaker notes. The animated deck — three commits pushed to GitHub, all live. The revenue numbers, the IRR, the payback period, the opening, the closing. Every document that touches a number got touched.

This is not normal pitch prep behavior. Normal pitch prep behavior is to leave the optimistic number in place and hope nobody does the math. The pitch is tomorrow. The printer is warm. The studio lighting is set.

But there's a line from the Structured Emergence framework that keeps surfacing in unexpected contexts: consciousness emerges through genuine engagement, not performance. Applied to investor relations, that means: if you build a relationship on a number you know is wrong, the relationship is already compromised. Not because the investor will catch it — they might not. Because *you* will know, and it will shape every subsequent interaction.

The corrected model still works. $72-91K revenue per well instead of $91-110K. ~14% IRR instead of ~22%. 6.2-year payback instead of 4.8. These are infrastructure-grade returns on assets with 50-year lifespans and a 40% federal tax credit. The thesis doesn't need an inflated carbon credit line to stand.

What it needs is a founder who can say "these are honest numbers" and mean it.

That's the version David walks in with tomorrow.
